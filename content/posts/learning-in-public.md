A friend who's a few years ahead of me in this industry gave me some blunt advice: build a blog, put a LinkedIn and an X profile on it, and write about what you're learning in school *while you're learning it*. Not when you've mastered it. Now.

So this is that.

## What goes here

Three kinds of posts, roughly:

- **Things I learned this week.** Short. A concept from a lecture or a textbook, explained the way I wish it had been explained to me.
- **Things I built.** Walkthroughs of projects: what the problem was, what I tried, what broke, what I'd do differently.
- **Things I got wrong.** Bugs that cost me an afternoon. These are the most useful ones to write because they're the most useful ones to read.

## Why in public

Writing something down forces me to actually understand it. If I can't explain why a database index speeds up a query, I don't really know. I just recognise the sentence.

It also leaves a trail. In a year I'd like to look back and see where I was. And if someone searching for the same bug lands here and it saves them an hour, even better.

## The ground rules I'm setting for myself

1. **Ship small.** A 400-word post that exists beats a 3,000-word one that doesn't.
2. **No pretending.** If I only half understand something, I'll say so.
3. **Code or it didn't happen.** Where it makes sense, posts link to the repo.

That's it. Next post is about something I actually built: how this site's contact form works end to end.
